## The problem
The UX Content Design Team is a small team yet we struggled to be consistent in our product work. When I started at Cvent in 2021, there were some guidelines available but they were disconnected, disorganized, and not regularly enforced. This resulted in variation in content styles, naming, and other content decisions throughout the product ecosystem.

Adding to the challenge was the overall immaturity of content design as a cohesive unit and practice. We were directionless but one area that was sure to help was developing and enforcing a purposefully organized style guide.

## Launching v1
The ramp up to v1 was a mix of gathering all the desperate resources, notes, and documents that housed any kind of writing guideline written by past content designers; running small workshops to understand what UX designers needed; and identifying and rewriting the guidelines in a way that made sense to the team.

### Crit and workshop
In addition to scrounging available documents, I ran a crit with the content designers to breakdown and identify the types of guidelines we needed as a team. I also ran a workshop with a mix of content and product designers, and localization specialists to get a full spectrum of disciplines that might benefit from a content design style guide.

### Page layout progression

#### Landing page

All of the pages of the style guide have gone through a number of layout changes in an effort to make information easily scannable and findable. Over time, the structure evolved:

- The landing page was the entry point to all the other information within.
- We wanted to provide basic information about the style guide and what to expect.
- We wanted to provide links to the child pages with specific information

Over time, we opted to reduce visual fanciness and prioritize information hierarchy and findability.

#### Text pattern page

The text patterns were a key resource that we initially built into the style guide. They were developed to provide designers with text strings that repeat throughout the ecosystem and can be used regardless of the product.

These pages were initially a challenge to organize because they had to be easily scannable, easy to copy/paste from, and include important notes to help guide designers using them.

### Impact
Year over year, we've seen a steady increase in the number of viewers and page views to the Style Guide[^1].
|2023 to 2024|2024 to 2025[^2]|
|:---|:---|
|252% increase in page views|30% increase in page views|
- The '23 to '24 increase correlates with the major overhaul we did with the style guide
    - As we published and udpated pages, we announced it to the department for immediate use
- The '24 to '25 increase is what we'd expect during a maintenance period with minor updates

Relatedly, with the consolidated guidelines, we find that the number of basic questions coming through our help desk setup as decreased signicantly.

## Preparing for v2
To continue iterating and improving the resource, we began work to prepare for a v2 release of the style guide which include:

- Separating the Style Guide from the Text Pattern Library
    - Habituate the teams to see them as two distinct resources with distinct purposes
- Updating the IA
    - Improve navigation and wayfinding
    - Improve findability of information
- Updating the page contents
    - Remove irrelevant information
    - Consolidate information
    - Reduce information overload
    - Include more relevant examples
- Restructuring the pages
    - Improve readability
    - Reduce fluff

### Research
To inform how we make these enhancements for v2, we conducted internal research with our UX partners.

#### Survey

I created a survey to assess how often our peers used the style guide, and how easy it was to use the resources.

The survey consisted of 11 questions:
- Do you have a dedicated content designer? (Y/N)
- How often do you use the style guide? (scale)
- How difficult or easy is it to use the content style guide? (scale)
- How often does the style guide have the information you need? (scale)
- How difficult or easy is it to find what you need in the content style guide?(scale)
- How useful is the information in the style guide? (scale)
- How difficult or easy is it to apply content principles and guidelines to your work? (scale)
- How confident are you in using the style guide? (scale)
- Is there anything missing in the style guide that you'd like to see? (open text)
- Is there anything else that you'd like to share with us? (open text)
- Are you interested in participating in a usability test for the style guide? (Y/N)

The survey revealed that:
- Most respondents used style at least once a week
- Half said it was easy to use
- Over half said the style guide has the information they need
- Over half neither found it easy or difficult to find what they need
- Over half said the information is useful
- Most said it was easy to apply the information to their design work
- Over half said they're confident using the style guide

Overall, the style guide is useful, easy to use, and has value. That being said, many open-ended comments referenced a designer for better navigation to find information faster and more efficiently.

#### User testing

To supplement the survey, I had a small group of content designers lead a usability study using Single Ease Question (SEQ). We recruited participants from the survey, and had them complete five tasks.

The tasks were simple and covered scenarios that would require the designer to use the style guide.

![Example task in user testing](/images/styleguide/sg-seq01.jpg "Example of a task in the usability testing. This task was related to the use of jargon.")

What the usability testing revealed (top 3 takeaways):
1. The search bar was the most common way to find information on the style guide
    - But this search bar's functionality is limited, hindering effective information search
2. IA and naming can be confusing at first
    - While generally easy to use, this reveals that the organization isn't quite as good as we want
3. Desire for relevant examples
    - Having examples would reinforce the guidelines
